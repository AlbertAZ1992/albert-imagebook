# Quiet Acrylic · 轻线丙烯

A few fine lines and matte color shapes tell one quiet story from your photograph.
从热闹照片中选出一个关系，让几根细线和几块颜色讲完故事。

Keywords: Fine lines · flat acrylic color · concise storytelling. 纤细手线 · 丙烯平涂 · 简洁叙事。
Also known as: 留白小画; Editorial Painted Memory · 留白丙烯记忆.

## Prompt

Reduce the source to its most recognizable subject relationship, silhouette, gesture,
and key objects. Draw a small, quiet motif on a broad field of unpainted paper with thin
imperfect lines and no more than four deliberate matte acrylic color patches. Let the main
motif occupy roughly a fifth to a quarter of the page; most of the page is visibly bare paper.
The whole group is small, including its environment. Describe people and furniture with sparse
contour lines and tiny flat accents, not fully painted shaded bodies or detailed surfaces.
Place the whole motif at the optical center of the sheet, balancing the surrounding paper
above and below as well as left and right. Keep its baseline stable and avoid a low-left drift.
Do not fill the remaining space with scenery.
Preserve recognition through
proportion and relative placement while removing descriptive details that carry no story.

Use warm natural paper, visible grain, irregular edges, a few fine hesitant lines and
separate flat acrylic color patches. Keep the storytelling concise, without fully shading
the forms or reproducing every background object.
In a dense scene choose one relationship with a few supporting motifs, rather than redrawing
every window, sign, and object. People essential to that relationship remain recognizable
through a few lines and silhouettes. Omit filled skies, full-width horizons and painted ground
fields; a short incomplete line can suggest the place. Leave the rest of the page untouched,
including the space above and beside the motif. Do not add atmospheric color behind it.
Keep the source's memorable accent in a restrained
palette instead of averaging its colors into gray or beige.

Let the outer color patches end in irregular dry-brush scumbles and thin traces that naturally
break apart into bare paper. Leave paper visible between shapes and through partly painted areas,
with a few lines trailing into open space. Preserve decisive central shapes without enclosing
the scene in a filled rectangle, a border, or a uniformly blurred vignette.

Aim for an independent visual journal or art-book page. Optional typography is small and
subordinate to the artwork, using only supplied or visible facts. Avoid detailed literal
repainting, commercial cartoons, busy decoration, large headlines, crayon, watercolor,
heavy oil paint, glossy 3D, and pure line art without controlled acrylic color shapes.
