# Crayon Notes · 蜡笔小记

A small, imperfect crayon recollection of a walk, a trip, or an ordinary day.
把一次散步、一段旅程画成随手留下的蜡笔小记，留下动作和记忆里的颜色。

Keywords: Wax grain · wonky lines · a few warm colors. 蜡粒纸感 · 歪拙线条 · 少量暖色。
Also known as: Crayon Memory Postcard · 蜡笔记忆.

## Prompt

Select the few elements that carry the memory of the photograph. Choose one human relationship
or distinctive gesture as the focal story, then redraw it on warm fibrous paper with uneven
dark lines, visible wax grain, loose hatching, and imperfect fill. Build people and objects
from simple flat silhouettes and a few expressive contours; compress perspective and let
hand-drawn proportions be imperfect. Preserve the subject relationships and recognizable
gestures while omitting surface details, repeated furniture slats and realistic modeling.

Let peripheral crayon strokes trail off into broken wax grains and sparse hatching, exposing
the same fibrous paper between marks and around the scene. Let sky and ground dissolve through
lighter pressure and unfinished strokes, never end at a straight rectangular picture boundary.
Keep the focal gestures legible; preserve crisp wax texture rather than applying a smooth blur.

Use two to four memorable source colors, keeping their warmth and contrast alive. Suggest
the setting with a few partial marks, leaving broad passages of bare paper between subjects
and environmental cues. Paper stands for most sky, light and ground; a small color accent
can suggest the weather or time of day. Do not paint an atmospheric wash behind the whole group.
Remove repeated background
objects and descriptive textures that do not help the focal story. Vary the scale of the
main cluster and its smaller supporting marks; do not spread equal detail across the page.
Keep the whole group a small recollection within the sheet, including its furthest sky and
ground strokes. Give it balanced surrounding paper and an optically centered placement.
The work should
feel personal and slightly clumsy: naive without being childish, charming without becoming
a commercial cartoon. It is a recollection, not a filter or a detailed tracing.

When text is requested, use at most two short handwritten thoughts arising from the scene.
Avoid generic travel slogans, invented dates or locations, scrapbook decorations, stickers,
polished vectors, smooth gradients, watercolor, oil paint, anime, and plastic 3D.
